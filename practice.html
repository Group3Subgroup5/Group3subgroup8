Worked example

Worked Here's the standard method for a cosine-based problem.

Problem: A right-angled triangle has an angle of 35°, with the side adjacent to it measuring 8 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse.

Step 1 — identify what's known and unknown: angle = 35°, adjacent = 8 cm, hypotenuse = ?

Step 2 — adjacent and hypotenuse → use cosine (CAH):

cos(35°) = 8 / h ⇒ h = 8 / cos(35°) ≈ 9.77 cm

Questions 1 – 5

Q1. A right triangle has angle θ = 28° and hypotenuse 12 cm. Find the side opposite θ.

HintOpposite and hypotenuse are involved — which ratio uses SOH?

Q2. A right triangle has adjacent = 6 cm and opposite = 9 cm. Find θ to the nearest degree.

HintOpposite over adjacent means TOA. You'll need tan¹&supminus;¹ on your calculator.

Q3. A ladder 5 m long rests against a wall, reaching 4.2 m up. Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground.

HintThe 4.2 m is opposite the angle; the ladder itself is the hypotenuse — use SOH, then sin&supminus;¹.

Q4. From a point 20 m from the base of a flagpole, the angle of elevation to the top is 40°. Find the height of the flagpole.

HintDraw the triangle first: the 20 m is adjacent, the height is opposite — that's TOA.

Q5. A right triangle has angle θ = 60° and the side adjacent to it is 5 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

HintAdjacent and hypotenuse — that's CAH.
