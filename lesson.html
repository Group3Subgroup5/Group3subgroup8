Lesson Content

Naming the sides, deriving the three ratios, and reading them off a reference triangle.

1. Naming the sides

In any right-angled triangle, once we fix an angle θ (other than the right angle), the three sides take on names relative to that angle:

  • Hypotenuse — the longest side, opposite the right angle. It never changes name.
  • Opposite — the side directly across from angle θ.
  • Adjacent — the side next to angle θ that is not the hypotenuse.

Swap which angle you're looking from, and "opposite" and "adjacent" swap too — only the hypotenuse stays fixed.

2. The three ratios — SOH CAH TOA

For angle θ in a right-angled triangle:

sinθ = Opposite / Hypotenuse


cosθ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse


tanθ = Opposite / Adjacent

The mnemonic SOH–CAH–TOA encodes this: Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse; Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse; Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent.

3. Worked example

Worked

A ladder leans against a wall, making an angle of 52° with the ground. The ladder is 4 m long. How high up the wall does it reach?

The height h is opposite the 52° angle; the ladder (4 m) is the hypotenuse. So we use sine:

sin(52°) = h / 4 ⇒ h = 4 × sin(52°) ≈ 3.15 m

Educational Design

Learning Indicators

B7/SHS: Learners can identify the sides of a right-angled triangle relative to a given angle and apply sine, cosine and tangent ratios to solve for unknown sides and angles.

Success Criteria

  • I can label opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse for any marked angle.
  • I can choose the correct ratio (SOH, CAH or TOA) for a given problem.
  • I can compute an unknown side or angle to 2 decimal places / nearest degree.

Teaching & Learning Resources

Scientific calculators, GeoGebra applet, printed ratio table, whiteboard triangle model, projector.

Core Competencies

Critical thinking & problem solving, digital literacy, communication & collaboration (paired ladder problems), personal development (self-checking with the calculator).
